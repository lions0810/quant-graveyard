## Pre-registered question and kill criteria

When a weekend deviation is large, does Monday trading continue or reverse it, and by how much? Is the weekend different from weekday overnight trading? The registered event requires absolute deviation ≥ 2%. This is descriptive research only, not a strategy backtest; no numerical predictive-effect kill threshold is stated. Size buckets and subgroup splits are descriptive, not parameter searches.

## Data scope

Use the 24 research-eligible Pionex spot instruments from the feasibility study, excluding liquidity and data-quality failures. The 4 leveraged-ETF underlyings remain but are separately examined. Reuse the existing 60M bars collected on 2026-10-29 and Yahoo adjusted daily prices; history begins with listings in 2025-09. The last complete Monday is 2026-10-26. There are 1,026 weekend observations, 200 large deviations across 51 Mondays, and 800 large weekday-overnight controls. The current unfinished weekend is excluded.

## Method

Define weekend deviation D from Friday's 16:00 ET close to Monday's 09:00 ET premarket close, and Monday return M from that premarket price to Monday's close. Apply ET daylight-saving conversion rather than fixed UTC hours. Require consecutive Friday and Monday trading dates, excluding holiday Mondays. Recovery ratio R is −M/D: positive means convergence, 1 means full recovery, and negative means continuation. Emphasize medians, quantiles and threshold proportions rather than unstable ratio means.

Compare weekdays, years, instruments, leverage groups and deviation-size groups; equally weight Mondays to address cross-instrument clustering. Premarket snapshots are not guaranteed executable prices, and actual opening-price execution and full trading costs are not tested. Compare tracking with the underlying, not performance with the desk's live system.

## Decisive results

| Group | Observations | Convergence | Continuation | Median R | Recovery ≥ half | Full recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large weekend deviations | 200 | 56.0% | 41.5% | 0.06 | 26.5% | 12.5% |
| Large weekday-overnight deviations | 800 | 48.5% | 51.2% | −0.03 | 26.2% | 11.8% |

The weekend-event Monday mean / median return is +0.40% / −0.00%. Equal weighting across 51 Mondays reduces the mean convergence proportion to 52.3%. Yearly convergence changes from 46.7% in 2025 to 58.7% in 2026. The daylight-saving correction changes the large-deviation set: 200 corrected events versus 199 fixed-hour events, with intersection 184. Monday full-period tracking-error standard deviation is 0.18% across the large-deviation sample.

## Verdict and what was not built

Large weekend deviations do not reliably predict Monday direction. The pooled convergence tilt weakens after Monday weighting, changes across years and does not imply substantial recovery. Weekday controls have nearly identical half- and full-recovery proportions. No strategy, survivor tag or handoff to the strategy builder; the line is closed. The weekday-overnight control is not a substitute for a separate complete overnight study.
