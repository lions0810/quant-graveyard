## Pre-registered question and kill criteria

Does a larger late-weekend volume share predict a larger Monday gap, and does late-window direction add information about that gap? Also examine advance-observable early-weekend volume and subsequent session convergence. The original report specifies descriptive research only, with no numerical strategy-promotion threshold. The follow-up separately asks whether Monday 09:00 ET quotes are active and quoted spread costs are small relative to typical gaps; it is not a strategy test.

## Data scope

The main report uses Pionex 60M bars and Yahoo prices for 24 research-eligible instruments: 9 historically weekend-active instruments and 15 frozen controls. Weekends from 2025-09-12 through 2026-11-06 supply 1,050 observations, comprising 495 active and 555 frozen. Zero-total-volume weekends are excluded. The latest Monday is incomplete at collection and contributes only premarket outcomes, not closing returns or convergence. The follow-up measures AAPL, AMZN, CRCL, GOOGL, META, NVDA, QQQ, SPY and TSLA on 2026-11-16 around 09:00–09:06 ET.

## Method

Use daylight-saving-corrected ET times. The late window runs from Sunday 12:00 to Monday 09:00; its primary share uses instrument units, with dollar turnover as a robustness check. R_early divides dollar turnover realized before Sunday 12:00 by Friday session turnover and is observable before the outcome. The reported gap G runs from Friday 16:00 to Monday 09:00, not the actual opening auction; session return runs onward to 16:00. Recovery is measured relative to G, with absolute G ≥0.5% for convergence comparisons.

Compare thirds, years, within-instrument relationships, gap signs and ratio-tail exclusions. The late-window share completes at the same time as G, making its apparent directional agreement partly mechanical. The follow-up reuses historical R_early cutpoints and takes best-bid/ask depth snapshots about 5 minutes apart. Neither study constructs a strategy equity curve or compares performance with the desk's live system.

## Decisive results

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 165 | 1.072% | 0.795% | 63.8% |
| Middle | 165 | 1.097% | 0.733% | 46.8% |
| High | 165 | 1.008% | 0.669% | 36.2% |

Late-window direction agrees with G in 92.5% of eligible observations, but the late window contributes median 77.2% of the weekend's absolute log-price movement. This is not independent gap prediction. The late-share convergence gradient is year-dependent. R_early's low/high convergence proportions are 29.0% / 61.8%, with yearly differences +41.0pp in 2025 and +32.1pp in 2026; removing ratios >5 leaves the gradient. This is a descriptive, post-selected relationship without out-of-sample trading validation, not a survivor.

## Live feasibility follow-up

The follow-up's final conclusion is "Executable," specifically for quote activity and quoted spread cost, not profitability. No instrument is frozen across the snapshots: 0/9. Quoted round-trip spread cost has median $0.52 per $10,000 notional; the highest is $13.27 in the initial snapshot and $7.57 in the later snapshot. Best-level displayed depth ranges from $83k to $162k. These are static best-level quotes, not measured fills, complete trading costs or guarantees against quote movement.

R_early is unusually elevated that weekend: median 5.49 and 8/9 instruments in the historical high third. The follow-up attributes this episode to large early turnover rather than uniformly thin Friday turnover; high/low labels provide little cross-instrument discrimination. The Monday close has not yet occurred, exit-side spreads are unmeasured, and the high-ratio tail remains unvalidated. The follow-up narrows the original execution-data gap but does not supply a profitable strategy or an out-of-sample convergence result.

## Verdict and what was not built

The primary gap-prediction hypothesis is negative; the early-volume convergence pattern remains descriptive. The follow-up supports active premarket quotes and small displayed spreads for this observation window, not general execution or trading profitability. No strategy, WATCH tag, survivor tag or handoff to the strategy builder; the line remains closed. Any operational extension requires separate approval, complete entry/exit and cost definitions, out-of-sample trading validation and a separate test of the high-ratio tail.
