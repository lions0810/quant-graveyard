## Pre-registered question and kill criteria

Is the observed small closing premium persistent, or an artifact of price adjustment or timing? Separate price-level premium from daily tracking error. The source specifies descriptive research only and no numerical effect-size kill threshold.

## Data scope

Pionex 60M bars and Yahoo daily prices for the 24 research-eligible instruments, including 4 leveraged-ETF underlyings. The sample covers listings from 2025-09 through 2026-10-30: 5,741 instrument-trading-days across 290 trading days. Daily panel statistics require at least 10 instruments. Completed new closes replace earlier partial observations. Intraday snapshots are supporting evidence, not a historical synchronized-price dataset.

## Method

Use daylight-saving-corrected 16:00 ET closes. Pc compares the instrument close with the underlying's unadjusted close; Pa uses its adjusted close as an artifact control. Daily tracking error compares daily returns with adjusted underlying returns and is not a price-level premium. Inspect subperiods, individual instruments and leverage groups. Dividend adjustment can inflate historical Pa, while closing-auction versus last-trade timing remains a possible residual confounder. No strategy returns or arbitrage execution are tested.

Daily tracking error has median −0.000% and positive proportion 50.0%. The October non-leveraged group has positive Pc proportion 88.0%. The elevated price level is a shared regime, not daily excess-return drift; the late-October snapshot is not the full-history norm.

## Verdict and what was not built

The premium is intermittent and regime-dependent, not a persistent markup. Adjusted historical prices must not be used to estimate this level premium. Its cause is unverified, and a premium larger than spread does not establish shorting, redemption or arbitrage feasibility. No strategy, survivor tag or handoff to the strategy builder; the line is closed. Performance is not compared with the desk's live system.
